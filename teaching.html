Teaching

Helping students become confident readers and writers.

Across six years, I've taught multilingual and first-year students in U.S. high school classrooms, intensive English programs, first-year writing, and a college-readiness program, and I've supported graduate courses in applied linguistics. What connects these rooms is a commitment to helping students read closely, write with purpose, and bring their full linguistic and cultural resources into academic work.

Courses taught

DSP Critical Analysis

UMass Boston · Directions for Student Potential · Summer 2026 · Instructor

About the program

DSP is a free six-week summer program for incoming first-year students. Successful completion guarantees admission to most majors at UMass Boston in the fall. The program combines critical reading and writing, quantitative reasoning, embedded tutoring, and community building.

About the course

Critical Analysis prepares students for the reading, writing, reasoning, and discussion expected in first-year college work. It takes its learning outcomes from CRW 112 and ENGL 101, and builds the core academic habits students need before first-year writing: careful reading, evidence-based interpretation, inquiry, discussion, drafting, feedback, revision, and reflection.

By the end of the course, students can

  1. Read short texts closely, identifying claims, key ideas, evidence, assumptions, and implications.
  2. Develop a focused argumentative claim in response to a text, issue, or inquiry question.
  3. Support an argument with relevant evidence from readings, course materials, or limited research.
  4. Explain and respond to an opposing view through counterargument and rebuttal.
  5. Organize an argumentative essay with a clear introduction, body paragraphs, transitions, and conclusion.
  6. Use feedback to revise for clarity, evidence, organization, and reasoning across drafts.
  7. Reflect on their growth as readers, writers, and college learners.

ENG 103: Rhetoric and Writing

Ball State University · Fall 2021 – Spring 2024 · Graduate Teaching Assistant (Instructor)

About the course

Ball State's first-year writing course introduces students to diverse rhetorics, writing processes, research, and information literacy. Students examine how language practices vary across cultural and rhetorical contexts, and create both written and multimodal texts.

Course goals

  • Identify and analyze rhetorical concepts from different cultures.
  • Reflect on and adjust their own composing strategies.
  • Recognize how grammar, citation, and genre conventions change across disciplines and cultures.
  • Engage critically with others' perspectives and language practices.
  • Compose texts in several formats for different purposes and audiences.
  • Use evidence from several sources to support a sustained argument.

What I did

  • Developed syllabi and course projects aligned with departmental objectives, including research-based, rhetorical-analysis, and multimodal projects.
  • Led class sessions, facilitated discussion, and guided peer-review workshops.
  • Evaluated student writing with department rubrics and gave detailed, revision-focused feedback.
  • Met students in office hours and writing conferences to support revision.

Intensive English Institute, Ball State University · Spring 2024 · Language Instructor

About the course

Level 4 is an upper-intermediate level focused on critical thinking and more demanding academic work. In the speaking course, students learn to take part in and lead academic classroom discussions, with attention to culturally appropriate interaction and pronunciation. Academic Bridge students are conditionally admitted to Ball State and take IEI classes alongside courses in their degree program.
