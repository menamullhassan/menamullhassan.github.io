About this tool

Start with what you want your chart to say, and the choice of chart gets much smaller. Familiar charts such as lines, columns, and bars are usually the easiest for readers to compare. Less common ones, such as slope or arrow plots, can make a point stand out but take a moment longer to read.

The colors in the Bright set were tested so that neighboring colors stay distinct for readers with the most common types of color blindness. Every chart with more than one color also has a key or labels, so color is never the only clue.

The chart types and the advice for each are inspired by Lisa Charlotte Muth’s A friendly guide to choosing a chart type on the Datawrapper blog.
