APA 7, made simple

A plain-language guide to APA Style (7th edition) for student papers: page setup, in-text citations, headings, the reference list, and the hanging indent, plus a reference builder for your own sources.

1. General format2. In-text citations3. Headings4. References5. Hanging indent6. Reference builder
1

General format

Set these once at the start and they apply to the whole paper.

8.5 × 11 instandard letter-size page
Doublespacing throughout, including the title page and reference list, with no extra space between paragraphs
1 inchmargins on all four sides
One fontused throughout, e.g. 12-pt Times New Roman, 11-pt Calibri, or 11-pt Arial
Top rightpage number on every page, with the title page as page 1
0.5 inchindent for the first line of each paragraph

The four main parts

A student paper has up to four parts, and each one starts on a new page.

1

Title page

  • The title three or four lines down from the top of the page: centered, bold, in Title Case.
  • One blank double-spaced line, then your name, department and university, course number and name, instructor, and due date, each centered on its own line (not bold).
  • Student papers need no running head.
2

Abstract

  • Only if your instructor asks for one.
  • The label Abstract on the first line, centered and bold.
  • One paragraph, not indented, usually no more than 250 words.
  • Then a new line, indented 0.5 inch, with the label Keywords: in italics followed by a few keywords (not italic).
3

Main body

  • Repeat the paper title on the first line: centered and bold.
  • Do not add the heading “Introduction.”
  • Indent each paragraph 0.5 inch. Sections follow each other without page breaks.
  • Organize sections with the heading levels.
4

References

  • Starts on a new page with the label References on the first line, centered and bold.
  • Entries in alphabetical order, double-spaced, with a hanging indent.
  • See the reference examples.

Your instructor’s guidelines always take priority over APA Style.

2

In-text citations

Every source you use gets a short citation in your sentence and a full entry in your reference list. There are two ways to write the citation.

Parenthetical

Author and year go in parentheses, usually at the end of the sentence.

… how speakers perceive color (Deutscher, 2011).

Narrative

The author is part of your sentence, with the year in parentheses after the name.

Deutscher (2011) argued that …

Common cases

Quotations(García, 2023, p. 6)

Add the page number: p. for one page, pp. for a range.

Pattern
“Quote” (Lastname, year, p. #).
Lastname (year) wrote that “quote” (p. #).
Examples
“Language is not simply a static system of syntactic, semantic, and phonetic rules” (García, 2023, p. 6).
García (2023) argued that “translanguaging pedagogical practices must respond to the ways of languaging of bilinguals” (p. 9).

For a quote of 40 words or more, use a block quote: start it on a new line, indent the whole quote 0.5 inch, leave out the quotation marks, and put the citation after the final period.

Summary or paraphrase(Canale & Swain, 1980)

The author and year are enough. A page number is optional but helpful.

Pattern
Your summary (LastnameA & LastnameB, year).
LastnameA and LastnameB (year) + your summary.
Examples
Communicative competence includes grammatical, sociolinguistic, and strategic competence (Canale & Swain, 1980).
Canale and Swain (1980) divided communicative competence into grammatical, sociolinguistic, and strategic competence.

With two authors, write & inside parentheses and and in your sentence.

Three or more authors(Conrad et al., 2020)

Write the first author’s last name and et al. in every citation, including the first one.

Pattern
Your sentence (LastnameA et al., year).
LastnameA et al. (year) + your sentence.
Examples
Multilingualism, interdisciplinarity, and technology run as themes through the whole field of applied linguistics (Conrad et al., 2020).
Conrad et al. (2020) designed their introduction to show students what it is like to do applied linguistics.

Editors are cited the same way as authors, as in this edited book.

Organization as author(ACTFL, 2024)

Write the organization’s full name. If it has a well-known short form, give the short form the first time you cite it, then use only the short form.

Pattern
First time: (Full Name [ABBR], year) or Full Name (ABBR, year)
After that: (ABBR, year) or ABBR (year)
Examples
The 2024 update revised the ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines 2012 (American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages [ACTFL], 2024).
The American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages (ACTFL, 2024) released revised proficiency guidelines in 2024.
The update aimed to make the guidelines clearer and easier to use (ACTFL, 2024).
ACTFL (2024) also aligned the guidelines with its other key documents.

The first two examples show the first citation; the last two show later citations. The short form is only for your text: the reference list always uses the full name.

Authors with the same last name(N. C. Ellis, 2002)

When two first authors in your reference list share a last name, add their initials in every citation, even if the years differ.

Pattern
(A. Lastname, year; B. Lastname, year)
A. Lastname (year) … B. Lastname (year)
Examples
Input and frequency have long been central topics in second language acquisition research (N. C. Ellis, 2002; R. Ellis, 1994).
N. C. Ellis (2002) reviewed frequency effects in language processing, while R. Ellis (1994) surveyed the field of second language acquisition as a whole.
Same author, same year(Long, 1983a, 1983b)

Add a, b, and so on after the year, in your text and in your reference list. The letters follow the alphabetical order of the titles.

Pattern
(Lastname, yeara, yearb)
Lastname (yeara) … Lastname (yearb)
Examples
Native speakers adjust both their language and the conversation itself when talking with non-native speakers (Long, 1983a, 1983b).
Long (1983b) argued that these conversational adjustments help make input comprehensible.

In the reference list, “Long, M. H. (1983a). Linguistic and conversational adjustments…” comes before “Long, M. H. (1983b). Native speaker/non-native speaker conversation…” because the titles are in alphabetical order.

Several sources at once(Canale & Swain, 1980; Swain, 1985)

In parentheses, list the sources in the same order as your reference list (alphabetical), separated by semicolons. In a narrative sentence, you can name them in any order.

Pattern
(LastnameA, year; LastnameB, year)
Examples
Researchers have long debated what it means to communicate successfully in a second language (Canale & Swain, 1980; Firth & Wagner, 1997; Swain, 1985).
Canale and Swain (1980), Swain (1985), and Firth and Wagner (1997) all examined what successful communication in a second language involves.
No page numbers(ACTFL, 2024, para. 2)

When you quote a webpage or another source without page numbers, count the paragraphs and give the paragraph number. You can also name the section heading, or both.

Pattern
(Lastname, year, para. #)
(Lastname, year, Heading section)
(Lastname, year, Heading section, para. #)
Examples
First released in 1986, the ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines are “widely recognized as the global gold standard for assessing functional language ability” (ACTFL, 2024, para. 2).
ACTFL (2024, para. 2) described its guidelines as “widely recognized as the global gold standard for assessing functional language ability.”

Both examples quote the same sentence, which is in the second paragraph of the ACTFL webpage, so both cite para. 2. They use the short form ACTFL because the full name was given earlier (see Organization as author).

Headings

APA has five heading levels. Use only as many as your paper needs; short papers often need only Levels 1 and 2. All headings use Title Case and are double-spaced like the rest of the paper.

References

Every reference has four parts: who wrote it, when, what it is called, and where to find it.

AuthorDateTitleSource
Book(Deutscher, 2011)

Edited book(Conrad et al., 2020)

Conrad, S., Hartig, A. J., & Santelmann, L. (Eds.). (2020). The Cambridge introduction to applied linguistics. Cambridge University Press.

(Conrad et al., 2020) · Conrad et al. (2020)

Add (Ed.) for one editor or (Eds.) for more than one.

(Swain, 1985) · Swain (1985)

The chapter title is not italic; the book title is. Editors’ initials come before their last names.

Journal article(Canale & Swain, 1980)

(Canale & Swain, 1980) · Canale and Swain (1980)

The journal name and volume number are italic; the issue number in parentheses is not.

Dictionary (author is also the publisher)(Merriam-Webster, 2025)

Merriam-Webster. (2025). Merriam-Webster’s collegiate dictionary (12th ed.).

(Merriam-Webster, 2025) · Merriam-Webster (2025)

The organization is the author. When the author and the publisher are the same, leave the publisher out.

(King, 2025) · King (2025)

Hanging indent

In the reference list, the first line of each entry starts at the left margin and every line after it is indented 0.5 inch.

Canale, M., & Swain, M. (1980). Theoretical bases of communicative approaches to second language teaching and testing. Applied Linguistics, 1(1), 1–47. https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/I.1.1

Deutscher, G. (2011). Through the language glass: Why the world looks different in other languages. Picador.

  1. Select the references you want to indent.
  2. On the Home tab, click the small arrow in the corner of the Paragraph group to open the paragraph settings (on a Mac, you can also choose Format > Paragraph).
  3. On the Indents and Spacing tab, under Special, choose Hanging.
  4. Set By to 0.5″.
  5. Click OK.
  1. Select the references you want to indent.
  2. Open the Format menu.
  3. Choose Align & indent > Indentation options.
  4. Under Special indent, choose Hanging and type 0.5.
  5. Click Apply.
  1. Turn on the ruler if you do not see it: in Word, View tab > Ruler; in Google Docs, View > Show ruler.
  2. Select the references you want to indent.
  3. Drag the left indent marker to 0.5 inch. This moves every line.
  4. Drag the first line indent marker back to 0. Now only the first line starts at the margin.
